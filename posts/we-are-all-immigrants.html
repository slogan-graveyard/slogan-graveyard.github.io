“We’re all immigrants”

The comforting myth that flattens history to silence the present.

It is the ultimate conversation-killer. Deployed by the self-righteous in any argument about borders, capacity, or identity, the phrase “we’re all immigrants” acts as a moral flashbang. It’s designed to stun you into silence by implying that any concern about today’s migration is a denial of your own ancestry. It doesn’t engage with your argument; it simply erases it.

The slogan relies on a premise that is technically true but practically useless. Yes, human populations move. Yes, the DNA of the British Isles is a mess of overlapping arrivals. But the slogan deliberately collapses the distinction between migration and invasion.

The Romans didn’t apply for visas; they marched legions. The Vikings didn’t seek a path to citizenship; they sought plunder and land. The Normans didn’t integrate into English society—they beheaded the leadership and rebuilt the landscape to suit their own needs. History is not a series of polite arrivals; it is a record of violent displacements. To equate the forced conversion of the Anglo-Saxons with modern managed immigration is a willful or ignorant distortion of what “migration” actually means.

Moreover, the slogan ignores the crushing weight of now. Your ancestors arriving a millennium ago does nothing to solve today’s housing shortage, the strain on the NHS, or the degradation of local wages. To claim that ancient history justifies modern chaos is a category error of the highest order. It is like arguing that you can’t fix your leaking roof because your great-grandfather built the house. The roof is still leaking, and you are the one who is going to get wet.

The irony is that this slogan is almost always deployed by the insulated. It is the language of the protected class—the homeowners, the well-connected, the people who view the world through the lens of academic abstractions. They can afford to be "inclusive" because their own lives are not impacted by the consequences of that inclusivity. They lecture the anxious from a position of absolute safety.

When this slogan is used, it is a signal that the speaker is not interested in your actual concerns. They are not interested in the realities of your neighborhood, your school, or your job. They are interested in the purity of their own narrative.

The truth is that a society is not a transit lounge. It is a project. It requires limits, standards, and a coherent identity to survive. To say "we're all immigrants" is to argue that there is no such thing as a host culture—only a permanent state of flux. But you cannot build a stable future on a foundation of flux.

It’s time to stop pretending that a slogan from the 1970s is a viable national strategy. Control is not cruelty. Discernment is not bigotry. And reminding people that you have a right to your own home doesn’t make you a xenophobe—it makes you a rational adult.
