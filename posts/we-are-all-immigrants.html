“We’re all immigrants”

a slogan that sounds generous but thinks for you

It’s everywhere. On protest signs, in opinion columns, deployed like a moral flashbang. The idea: if you go back far enough, everyone came from somewhere else. Therefore, opposing immigration is hypocrisy. Therefore, shut up.

There’s a tiny grain of truth buried under all that smugness. Yes, human populations have moved. The Romans arrived. The Angles and Saxons arrived. Vikings, Normans, Celts before them. Almost no one has been rooted to one patch of soil for eternity. But the slogan stretches that fact until it snaps.

History isn’t a warm hug. The Romans didn’t come with gift baskets and a desire for multicultural dialogue. They came with swords, taxes, and forts. The Normans showed up, killed the English king, and built castles on conquered land. The Vikings didn’t ask politely for assimilation. Even the so-called “native Britons” had displaced whoever lived there before. The slogan pretends all migration is the same gentle flow of curious travellers. It’s not. Most historical movement was conquest, flight, or desperation.

It flattens time and responsibility. My ancestors arriving a thousand years ago has zero bearing on immigration policy today. A Norman baron’s bloodline doesn’t mean my town should accept unlimited newcomers without planning. The past is not a blank cheque for the present. Saying “we’re all immigrants” is like saying “we’re all African” because humanity originated there. Technically true. Practically useless.

It ignores present pressures. Housing, wages, school places, NHS waiting times, social cohesion. These are not abstract historical debates. They are the daily reality of people who have no choice but to live here. The slogan is most often deployed by the insulated: older, house‑owning, financially comfortable people who feel no strain from rapid demographic change. They can afford to be kind. They lecture the anxious from a position of zero skin in the game.

It shuts down debate with a guilt trip. That’s the real function. Not to inform, but to silence. To frame any concern about pace, scale, or cultural fit as irrational bigotry. It’s a rhetorical cudgel, not an argument. And like most cudgels, it works best on people too polite to push back.

The truth is messier and less feel‑good. Some migration is good. Some is badly managed. Some newcomers integrate beautifully; some don’t. A country has every right to control its borders, set rules, and prioritise its own citizens’ welfare. That’s not racism. That’s sovereignty.

“We’re all immigrants” sounds clever in a tweet. But a slogan that can’t tell the difference between a fleeing refugee, a skilled worker, an invader from a thousand years ago, and a person who just wants a quieter street is a slogan that deserves a quiet burial.
