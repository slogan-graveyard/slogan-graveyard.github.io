The "Cash Poor" Paradox

When your house is a goldmine but your pockets are empty.

The most convenient excuse for a millionaire in need of care is that they are "cash poor." It is a phrase that attempts to divorce the reality of their assets from the reality of their income. The goal is simple: to maintain a high-value estate while having the state foot the bill for their basic needs.

This isn't a financial tragedy; it's a strategic choice. To claim you cannot afford care while sitting on a million-pound asset is to argue that the value of that asset belongs to your heirs, not to you. You are asking the taxpayer to subsidize your children's future inheritance.

If the state pays for your nursing home, they are effectively giving you an interest-free loan on your house. You get the care you need, and your family keeps the asset. In any other part of the economy, this would be called a windfall.

We must stop accepting the notion that "wealth" is only what you have in your bank account. Wealth is what you own. If you own the land, you are not poor—you are simply refusing to liquidate your position.

A house is an asset, not a sanctuary. When it becomes a burden to the public, it's time to turn that asset into the care you need. Inheritance is a gift, not a right.
