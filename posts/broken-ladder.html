The Broken Ladder

Why "working hard" is no longer a viable wealth strategy.

“Work hard, save your money, and buy a house.” It is the timeless advice of the previous generation. It is also an economic delusion. They are giving us a map of a city that no longer exists.

The ladder of social mobility was once built on a simple ratio: wages vs. the cost of housing. When the house price was three times your annual salary, hard work and saving could actually get you into the market. Today, that ratio is ten times—or more. The math is broken.

When we see young people qualifying for state assistance while awaiting a million-pound inheritance, we are seeing the new class divide. It is no longer about who works the hardest; it is about who is born into the right family. We are returning to a landed gentry system, where wealth is inherited and maintained, not earned.

The tragedy is that the "hard work" narrative is still used to shame the youth. They are told to "hustle" and "grind," while the entrance fee to the middle class has risen beyond the reach of anyone who doesn't already have a benefactor.

You cannot "bootstrap" your way into an asset class that requires a windfall to enter. The ladder isn't just missing a few rungs; it has been pulled up and brought inside.
